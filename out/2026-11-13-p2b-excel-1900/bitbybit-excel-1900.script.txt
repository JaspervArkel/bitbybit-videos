Excel thinks February twenty-ninth, nineteen hundred, was a real day.
It wasn't. Nineteen hundred was not a leap year.
Century years skip leap day unless they divide by four hundred.
Type it into Excel, and it's accepted as a date.
Why? In the eighties, the top spreadsheet was Lotus one-two-three,
and it treated nineteen hundred as a leap year.
Microsoft copied that, so Lotus files would open correctly.
Fixing it now would shift almost every date in existing spreadsheets by one day.
So Excel still keeps a day that never existed, on purpose.
